## What this is, in one paragraph

After M24.4b, a **settled** menu is still a flat rectangle with dark boxes and hard
frame lines over the room, which reads as a screen. This slice makes the settled menu
read as **something the eye perceives**, and makes the eye itself alive:
1. **Lens warp:** a slight barrel curve of the implant layer around the **viewport
   centre** (not the panel's centre), as if the menu lay on the curved inside of the eye.
2. **Light, not boxes:** no dark panel fills and no hard frame lines. The text is light
   that glows over the room, with a soft dark "cloud" behind it for legibility, and the
   panel's edge fades out irregularly.
3. **Peripheral softening:** sharp where the player looks (the gaze point) and at the
   screen centre; slightly soft, less saturated and colour-fringed towards the edges.
4. **Focus shift (depth of field):** the eye focuses at one distance at a time. Looking
   at the menu, the room blurs softly. Looking at the room or the hologram, the room
   sharpens at that depth and the menu softens.
5. **The eyes move:** when the player leaves the mouse alone, the gaze wanders around the
   room. It jumps (a saccade) from the hologram to the lamp, to the menu, to the slab
   rack. The focus follows each jump, and the view shifts by a fraction of a degree.
6. **Micro-tremor:** the implant layer drifts by about 0.3 px. You feel it more than you
   see it.
7. **The projector is bigger:** on the main menu, the projector plus its hologram
   covers about a fifth of the screen (today it is about a twentieth).
8. **The library looks into the slab rack:** a second, wall-mounted projector shows the
   selected variant large, and the slabs that variant would use slide out of their slots.

None of this is LED vocabulary: no scan lines, no square pixels, no hard steps (M24.4b
"What this is").

### 1.2 Lens warp

```cpp
/// Where to sample the undistorted implant layer for output pixel `px` (pixels, origin
/// top-left), for a barrel curve centred on the viewport. Centre fixed; content is
/// pulled towards the centre more the further out it is, so straight lines bow outward
/// and nothing at the edges is cropped:
///   d = (px - size/2) / (0.5 * length(size))        // r = |d| is 1 at the corners
///   src = size/2 + (px - size/2) * (1 + k * r*r)
/// The shader uses the same formula (keep the two in sync; the GPU test checks it).
[[nodiscard]] Vec2f lensWarpSource(Vec2f px, Vec2f size, float k) noexcept;
```
(Use the project's existing 2-D float vector type. Grep `struct Vec2` in `src/view/` and
`src/render/`. If there is none in `view`, use `std::array<float, 2>` and note it.)

**Tests:**
- the centre maps to itself, exactly;
- with `k = 0`, every point maps to itself;
- along any ray from the centre, `|src − c|` grows monotonically and
  `|src − c| ≥ |px − c|`; equality only at the centre;
- a corner of a 1920×1080 viewport samples about 4.5 % further out than itself
  (`1 + k` exactly at r = 1);
- symmetry: mirrored points map to mirrored points.

**Input direction (important).** The pixel the player sees at screen position `p` shows
layer content from `lensWarpSource(p)`. So ImGui must be told the mouse is at
`lensWarpSource(realMouse)`: Step 4.3.

### 1.4 Eccentricity

```cpp
/// How far `px` is from where the eye is sharp, in screen heights: the smaller of the
/// distance to the gaze point and 1.3x the distance to the screen centre (the centre of
/// the visual field stays relatively sharp even when the gaze is elsewhere).
[[nodiscard]] float eccentricity(Vec2f px, Vec2f gazePx, Vec2f size) noexcept;

[[nodiscard]] float peripheryAmount(float eccentricity) noexcept;
```
Tests: 0 at the gaze point and at the centre; 1 at a corner when the gaze is at the
opposite corner; monotonic along a line away from both.

### 1.5 The gaze model

The gaze is a small state machine stepped once per frame. It is **deterministic**: all
"random" choices come from `hash32(seed, fixationCount)` (grep the project's existing
integer hash, for example in `deco` or `fx`; reuse it).

```cpp
/// Something the eye may rest on. `world` is in renderer world units (cells; Z up).
struct GazeTarget {
  std::string_view key;   ///< "hologram", "menu", "lamp", ...
  Vec3f world{};          ///< ignored for key "menu" (the panel is not in the world)
  float weight{0};        ///< relative chance of being chosen while wandering
};

struct GazeInput {
  float dt{0};                   ///< seconds since the last step, clamped to [0, 0.1]
  Vec2f mousePx{};               ///< real (unwarped) mouse position
  bool mouseMoved{false};        ///< this frame
  bool mouseOverPanel{false};    ///< inside an implant panel rect (not a tool rect)
  bool dragging{false};          ///< any mouse button held
  bool active{false};            ///< living vision applies: settled lab menu, no move
  bool wander{true};             ///< the setting/CLI switch (captures default false)
  float intensity{1};            ///< presentation intensity
  Vec2f viewport{};              ///< pixels
  Vec2f panelCentrePx{};         ///< centre of the main implant panel, for key "menu"
  std::span<const GazeTarget> targets;
  /// Projects a world point to pixels and returns its eye distance (world units), or
  /// nullopt when behind the camera. main.cpp builds it from the camera of this frame.
  std::function<std::optional<std::pair<Vec2f, float>>(Vec3f)> project;
  float defaultDistance{1};      ///< world units: the hologram's distance, the fallback
  std::uint32_t seed{0};
};

struct GazeState {
  Vec2f pointPx{};               ///< where the eye looks now (eased through saccades)
  Vec2f fromPx{}, toPx{};        ///< the current saccade
  float saccadeT{1};             ///< 0..1, 1 = landed
  float uiFocus{1};              ///< 1 = focused on the implant panel, 0 = on the room
  float uiFocusTarget{1};
  float focusDistance{1};        ///< world units, eased in dioptres (1/d)
  float focusDistanceTarget{1};
  float idle{0};                 ///< seconds since the mouse last moved
  float fixationLeft{0};         ///< seconds until the next wander saccade
  std::uint32_t fixationCount{0};
  int lastTarget{-1};
  Vec2f eyeShift{};              ///< yaw, pitch radians added to the camera
  Vec2f eyeShiftTarget{};
};

[[nodiscard]] GazeState stepGaze(const GazeState& s, const GazeInput& in) noexcept;
```

Behaviour of `stepGaze`, in order:

1. **Not active** (`!in.active`), for example during a station move, on the game screen
   or with the lab off:
   - ease `uiFocus` to 1 and `eyeShift` to {0, 0} with the accommodation time constant;
   - set `pointPx` to the viewport centre; reset `idle`, `fixationLeft` and `saccadeT = 1`;
   - `focusDistanceTarget = defaultDistance`.
   Then return. (So a move always lands with the eye centred and focused on the menu.)
2. **Idle time:** `idle = mouseMoved || dragging ? 0 : idle + dt`.
3. **Mouse-driven** (`idle < kIdleBeforeWander`, or `!wander`, or calm intensity):
   - start a saccade to `mousePx` when it is more than 0.04 × viewport height from
     `toPx`; otherwise set `toPx = mousePx` directly (smooth pursuit);
   - `uiFocusTarget = mouseOverPanel ? 1 : 0`;
   - `focusDistanceTarget`: if over the panel, keep the previous target (the room's
     focus does not matter while reading). Otherwise, the eye distance of the target
     whose projection is closest to the mouse within 0.08 × viewport height, skipping
     "menu"; if none, `defaultDistance`;
   - `eyeShiftTarget = {0, 0}` (the player is steering with the mouse; no extra motion).
4. **Wandering** (`idle ≥ kIdleBeforeWander`, `wander`, intensity ≥ 0.5): when
   `fixationLeft ≤ 0`, pick the next target:
   - weighted by `weight`, with the hash of (`seed`, `fixationCount`), never the same
     index as `lastTarget`, and skipping targets whose projection is off-screen;
   - start a saccade to its pixel (the panel centre for "menu");
   - `uiFocusTarget = key == "menu" ? 1 : 0`;
   - `focusDistanceTarget` = its eye distance, or keep the previous value for "menu";
   - `eyeShiftTarget` = `kEyeShiftFraction` × the yaw/pitch angle from the viewport
     centre to the target's pixel (use the camera fov: `angle ≈ atan(offsetPx / f)`,
     where `f = 0.5 · height / tan(fov/2)`), clamped to `kEyeShiftMaxRadians` in
     magnitude; zero for "menu";
   - `fixationLeft` = lerp(`kFixationMin`, `kFixationMax`, hash01); `fixationCount++`.
   Otherwise `fixationLeft −= dt`.
5. **Saccade:** `saccadeT = min(1, saccadeT + dt / kSaccadeSeconds)`;
   `pointPx = mix(fromPx, toPx, smoothstep(saccadeT))`. Starting a saccade sets
   `fromPx = pointPx` and `saccadeT = 0`.
6. **Easing** with `a = 1 − exp(−dt / kAccommodationSeconds)`:
   - `uiFocus += (uiFocusTarget − uiFocus) · a`;
   - focus in dioptres: `inv = 1/focusDistance`;
     `inv += (1/focusDistanceTarget − inv) · a`; `focusDistance = 1/inv`;
   - `eyeShift` eases towards `eyeShiftTarget`, but with the **saccade** profile, not
     the exponential: it moves with `saccadeT`. Keep `eyeShiftFrom` in the state if you
     need it; add the field.
   - **Fixation drift:** add `kDriftRadians · (sin(0.37t + seed), sin(0.29t + 2seed))`
     to `eyeShift` while wandering only. Here t is the visual time; add `double time` to
     `GazeInput`.

Gaze target defaults are data, not code: Step 2.

**Tests** (feed synthetic inputs; `project` maps world (x, y, z) to pixels with any
simple linear function in the test):
- inactive → `uiFocus` approaches 1, `eyeShift` approaches 0 and `pointPx` is the centre;
- mouse over the panel → after 1 s, `uiFocus > 0.98`;
- mouse over the room near the lamp target → after 1 s, `uiFocus < 0.02` and
  `focusDistance` is within 2 % of the lamp's distance;
- **determinism:** two runs with the same seed and inputs give identical states for
  20 s of 60 Hz steps;
- **wandering:** after 2.5 s idle, a saccade starts within one step. Over 60 s it visits
  ≥ 3 distinct targets, never repeats the same target twice in a row, and every
  fixation lasts 1.2–3.2 s;
- `|eyeShift| ≤ kEyeShiftMaxRadians + kDriftRadians` always;
- a saccade lands in `kSaccadeSeconds` ± one step;
- `wander = false` → no saccade without mouse motion, and `eyeShift` stays 0;
- calm intensity (0) → no wandering and no eye shift.

### 1.6 Depth-of-field circle of confusion

```cpp
/// Blur radius in pixels (1080p) for a surface at eye distance `z` when the eye is
/// focused at `focus` (both world units > 0). Thin-lens in dioptres:
///   coc = clamp(|1/z - 1/focus| * kCocScale, 0, 1) * kWorldDefocusPixels
/// with kCocScale chosen so that focus on the menu (focus = kMenuFocusDistance) blurs
/// the hologram (about 1.0 m away at the menu station) by ~70 % of the maximum.

[[nodiscard]] float circleOfConfusion(float z, float focus, float cocScale) noexcept;
```

### 4.2 `implant.frag` (living vision, after M24.4b's step 1 "tools untouched")

When `living == 0`, the shader runs M24.4b's code unchanged. When `living == 1`:
```
// a. where to sample: lens warp, then tremor (tool pixels were already returned)
srcPx = c + (px - c) * (1 + warpK * r2)            // r2 from d = (px - c)/(0.5*length(size))
srcPx += tremor
uv    = srcPx / screenSize
if (uv outside [0,1]) -> sample nothing (vec4(0)) for the UI; the world shows through.
// b. M24.4b steps 2..8 run with `uv` in place of `fragUv`, and with the panel record
//    lookup using srcPx (records are in layer space).
// c. peripheral softening, settled pixels too:
e      = eccentricity(px, gaze, size)            // same formula as view::eccentricity
pa     = smoothstep(kPeripheryStart, kPeripheryFull, e)
radius = max(unsyncRadius, pa*kPeripheryBlurPixels*pixelScale,
             (1-uiFocus)*kUiDefocusPixels*pixelScale)
fringe = pa*kPeripheryFringePixels*pixelScale, radially (direction normalize(px - c)):
         R sampled at uv + dir*fringe/size, B at uv - dir*fringe/size, G and A at uv,
         each with blurSample(.., radius)
col    = mix(col, vec3(luma(col)), pa*kPeripheryDesaturate)
// d. glow: the text is light
halo   = blurSample(uv, 5*pixelScale).rgb
col   += halo * 0.30 * local
// e. cloud edge and veil, for the panel record covering this pixel (settled or not)
inside = signed distance into the record's rect (px > 0 inside)
edgeN  = (fbm(srcPx/(60*pixelScale) + seed) - 0.5) * 16*pixelScale
edge   = smoothstep(0, 26*pixelScale, inside + edgeN)
col   *= edge; a *= edge
scrim  = veil * smoothstep(-30*pixelScale, 30*pixelScale, inside + edgeN) * local
a      = max(a, scrim)      // premultiplied: alpha without colour darkens the room
// f. looking away
col   *= mix(kUiAwayAlpha, 1, uiFocus); a *= mix(kUiAwayAlpha, 1, uiFocus)
// g. M24.4b step 9 (heartbeat, dip) last, as before
```

### 4.4 Depth of field for the room

1. **Make depth readable.** In `OffscreenTarget`, add `VK_IMAGE_USAGE_SAMPLED_BIT` to the
   depth image's usage, **only if**
   `limits.sampledImageDepthSampleCounts & kSampleCount` (query once in `VulkanContext`;
   expose `bool depthSampleable()`). Make sure the depth attachment's `storeOp` is
   `STORE` on that path (around `board_renderer.cpp` line 2573); today it may be
   `DONT_CARE`. Keep it `DONT_CARE` when depth of field is off, so other paths cost
   nothing.
2. **New pass `dof.frag`**, run in place of the uniform defocus, only on the living path
   with `depthSampleable()`. It is separable and ping-pongs like the existing blur
   (`board_renderer.cpp` around lines 2865–2938; mirror its barriers, scratch image and
   descriptor pattern):
   - inputs: the colour (or scratch) image, the depth image as `sampler2DMS`, read with
     `texelFetch(depth, ipx, 0)` (sample 0 is fine for a blur);
   - push constants: direction, `near`/`far` or the projection terms needed to linearise
     depth (grep how `OrbitCamera` builds the projection: reversed Z or not, infinite far
     or not; write `linearDepth` to match and **unit-test the CPU mirror** of it against
     the camera's own projection of a few points), `focus` (world units), `cocScale`,
     `maxPx` (`kWorldDefocusPixels · pixelScale`);
   - per pixel: `coc(z_centre)`. Gather 9 taps along the direction at spacing
     `coc/4` px. Weight each tap by `step(tapCoc, centreCoc + 0.5) · w[i]`, which keeps
     sharp foreground edges from bleeding onto blurred background, a standard cheap
     approximation. Normalise by the weight sum. With `coc < 0.25` px, output the
     centre texel exactly;
   - layout transition for depth: `DEPTH_ATTACHMENT_OPTIMAL →
     DEPTH_READ_ONLY_OPTIMAL` (or `SHADER_READ_ONLY_OPTIMAL`) from the late fragment
     tests stage to the fragment shader, and back for the next frame's UNDEFINED → ...
     transition (already at line 2551; check that it still holds).
3. **Order:** scene → post (bloom/tone map) → **DoF** → implant layer → tools. The
   existing pause defocus (`blur_`) is a different feature: leave it alone. If both
   would run, the pause defocus wins and DoF is skipped.
4. **Fallback:** without `depthSampleable()`, approximate with the existing uniform
   defocus at strength `(1 − nearFocus)`, where nearFocus is 1 when the focus is beyond
   the hologram. Note in Status which path lavapipe takes.
5. **Interaction with the hologram:** the hologram material is emissive and maybe
   additive with no depth write (M24.3; check). If it does not write depth, it takes the
   depth of what is behind it and blurs with the wall. Then exclude it: render the
   hologram after the DoF pass, or give it its own depth write. Pick the smaller change,
   look, and note it.
