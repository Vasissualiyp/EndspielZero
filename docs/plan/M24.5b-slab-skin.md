## What this is

During a game, the board morphs between flat and its shape: cylinder, torus, Möbius and
Klein, with staged rolls. Today the cells simply bend, which reads as a picture changing
shape. In the lab, the LED face is a **stretch skin**: rigid LED tiles joined by thin
bridges on a stretchable membrane. When the board morphs:
1. The **tiles keep their size**. Where the shape stretches the sheet, the tiles part,
   and the membrane with its faintly glowing bridges shows in the gaps. Where it
   compresses, the gaps close.
2. **Ribs** grow from the mast's yoke and hold the skin from behind. They stay visible.
3. When two edges meet (a cylinder's or a torus's seam closing), **edge latches** click
   shut: a short hard-stepped flash runs along the seam in its axis colour.
4. Where the shape must pass **through itself** (the Klein bottle), the skin cannot. The
   cells in that band are drawn as **hologram cells** from the projector instead of LED
   tiles: light completes what 3-D cannot.

**Scope:** only on the lab game path (`labGame` from M24.5), only for the parametrised
2-D surfaces (cylinder, torus, Möbius, Klein, and any other `PlaySurface` that is not
`stacked()`). Stacked D ≥ 3 shapes (`torus3d`, `hyper4`) are M24.6's problem: leave them
unchanged. With the lab off, everything is byte-identical.

## Step 1 — per-cell stretch (pure, test first)

1. In `play_surface.hpp`, add:
   ```cpp
   /// The slab skin (M24.5b): how much each cell's footprint is stretched by the current
   /// embedding, relative to the flat board, along the files (u) and ranks (v).
   /// 1 = as flat; > 1 stretched; < 1 compressed.
   struct CellStretch { CellId cell{kInvalidCell}; float u{1}; float v{1}; };
   [[nodiscard]] std::vector<CellStretch> cellStretch(const PlaySurface& shaped,
                                                      const PlaySurface& flat);
   ```
   - The stretch along u is the **surface length** between the cell's two u-edge
     midpoints. Sample `playShapeAt` at `du = −0.5, 0, +0.5`, `dv = 0`, and sum the two
     chord lengths; this is accurate enough at 1/2-cell spacing. Divide it by the same
     measure on the flat pose. Likewise for v. The sampling helper is the same `at(du,
     dv)` lambda as the patch loop: factor it into a private function so both use one
     derivation.
   - The flat pose: the same variant with `formed = 0` (grep how `SurfacePose` carries
     `formed`). Cache the flat result per variant: it never changes.
2. **Tests** (`tests/render/test_play_surface.cpp` or a new `test_slab_skin.cpp`,
   `[render][skin]`):
   - every cell is 1 ± 1e-4 on the flat pose;
   - a fully formed cylinder has u or v (whichever axis rolls) ≈ 1 ± 2 %, because a
     cylinder is developable;
   - a fully formed torus has at least one cell > 1.15 on the outer equator and one
     < 0.9 on the inner, along the ring direction;
   - the stretch is continuous in `formed`: max change < 0.05 per 0.01 of `formed`.

## Step 2 — tiles keep their size; the membrane shows

1. **Shrunk tiles.** Add an option to `PlaySurface::build` (a field on `SurfacePose`,
   default off, so every existing call is unchanged): `bool skin{false}`. When on, each
   patch is sampled over a smaller footprint:
   `du *= min(1, 1/stretch.u)`, `dv *= min(1, 1/stretch.v)`, on top of `kCoverage`. So a
   stretched cell's tile stays the flat size, centred in its cell. A compressed cell is
   not enlarged; its tile shrinks with the sheet. The `cellStretch` needs to be known
   during the build: compute it first inside `build` when `skin` is on.
2. **The membrane.** When `skin` is on, also emit, per cell, the **unshrunk** patch (the
   current geometry), offset by **−0.025 cells along its corner normals** (behind the
   tile, away from the viewer side the piece stands on). It is a second surface layer:
   - store it in `PlaySurface` as `membranePatches()`, same type;
   - render it in `board_renderer.cpp` next to the tile patches, with **material matte
     (0)**, colour `#0B0F14`, and **no fx cell**: `fxCell = 0`, so the LED fx never
     touches it;
   - **bridges glow:** add an emissive term to its vertex colour: LED cyan
     `theme.ledOn` (grep the name the LED shader uses for the lit colour) at alpha
     `0.12 × smoothstep(1.02, 1.35, max(stretch.u, stretch.v))`. Multiply by the board's
     current power (grep how patches get their power/fx; the membrane must go dark when
     the board powers down);
   - a **serpentine hint**: in `board.frag`, only for the membrane (flag it by a
     dedicated `material` value 0.5, which is matte with a sub-flag; check that the
     fractional part of matte is unused, or pick another free encoding and note it),
     modulate the emissive by `0.5 + 0.5·sin(40·u_cell)`, where `u_cell` is the
     fractional lattice coordinate (pass it in the vertex's free channel, or derive it
     from `fxCell`'s neighbours). If no channel is free, skip the serpentine and note it.
3. **Picking is unchanged:** `PlaySurface::pick` must keep testing the **unshrunk**
   cells, so a click in a gap still picks the cell it belongs to. Add a test: with skin
   on, picking the screen point of a gap centre on a stretched torus returns the cell
   whose footprint contains it.
4. Set `skin = true` only when `labGame` is on and the board is not `stacked()`. Thread
   it through wherever the session builds its play surface (grep `PlaySurface::build(`).
   Prefer a `BoardOptions::slabSkin` flag set by `main.cpp`, like `labMode`.

## Step 3 — ribs

1. **Pure geometry** (`src/render/slab_skin.{hpp,cpp}`, new):
   ```cpp
   struct Rib { std::vector<view::Vec3> points; };   ///< a polyline, board space
   /// Ribs behind the skin for the current shape: `count` lattice lines across each
   /// rolled axis, sampled every half cell, offset 0.6 cells behind the skin (against
   /// the seat normal), plus two spokes from `yoke` to the nearest rib point of the two
   /// ribs closest to it. Empty when `formed` is 0.
   [[nodiscard]] std::vector<Rib> slabRibs(const PlaySurface& shaped, int count,
                                           const view::Vec3& yoke, float formed);
   ```
   - **Which lines.** For an axis that wraps in this variant (cylinder: one; torus and
     Klein: both; Möbius: the one along the band), take `count = 6` lattice lines of the
     **other** coordinate, evenly spaced (at `(k + 0.5)·size/6`). Each rib runs the full
     length of its line, around the roll. Use `playShapeAt` at those lattice values. An
     axis that does not wrap gets no ribs along it.
   - **Growth with the morph.** Each rib is visible from its middle outwards, over a
     fraction `smoothstep(0.0, 0.35, formed)` of its length. It grows out from the spoke
     end as the shape forms, and retracts as it flattens.
   - **Tests:**
     - no ribs at `formed = 0`;
     - every rib point is 0.6 ± 0.05 cells behind the nearest seat, measured along that
       seat's normal;
     - for a full torus, 12 ribs plus 2 spokes;
     - ribs never cross the playing side: for every point, the dot of (point − nearest
       seat centre) with the seat normal is < 0.
2. **Render** each rib as a chain of thin `cylinder` lab-mesh instances (Ø 0.012 m,
   material metal, colour `#2A2E35`) between consecutive points, in the board batch
   (`InstanceSet::lab`, already drawn on the lab path, in board space). The yoke point
   is the M24.5 yoke's centre for the board's (single) slab.
3. **Slab back stays.** The slab back and yoke from M24.5 stay where they are: the skin
   peels off the plate as it forms, and the spokes join plate and ribs. Do not move the
   back.

## Step 4 — seam latch flash

1. **When.** A wrap axis closes when `formed` crosses its closing value **upwards**:
   - cylinder: the end of the roll (`kTorusRollEnd` for the axis that rolls first, if the
     cylinder morph is staged that way; grep how cylinder stages use it);
   - torus: the roll axis at `kTorusRollEnd`, the ring axis at 1.0;
   - Klein: the ring at `kKleinRingEnd`;
   - Möbius: at 1.0.

   Find the exact per-shape stage table in `play_surface.cpp` (lines ~160–200 use these
   constants) and derive the closing value per axis from it. Put the result in a pure
   function `std::array<float, 2> wrapCloseAt(const VariantSpec&)`, with NaN for an axis
   that never closes, and test it.
2. **Event.** In `app::FxTimeline`, add `latch(axis, now)` and
   `std::optional<LatchFx> latchAge(double now)` (`axis`, `age`). `main.cpp` calls
   `latch` when the live `formed` value crosses `wrapCloseAt` upward between two frames.
   Opening never flashes.
3. **Look.** For 0.25 s after the latch, the rail on that axis (the `surfaceRail` weight
   for that axis's wrap ring) is boosted in **three hard steps**: ×2.5 for 0–0.06 s,
   ×1.6 for 0.06–0.14 s, ×1.2 for 0.14–0.25 s, then back to ×1. The colour is the seam's
   own (the rail colour). Hard steps are LED vocabulary: correct, because this is
   hardware. At calm intensity: no flash.
4. **Test:** `wrapCloseAt` for each shape; the step function's values at 0.03, 0.1, 0.2
   and 0.3 s; and a crossing in a sequence of `formed` values fires exactly once.

## Step 5 — the Klein light band

1. **Find the pass-through cells** (pure, `slab_skin.cpp`):
   ```cpp
   /// Cells whose skin would have to pass through another part of the skin: any patch
   /// corner closer than `gap` (cells) to a corner of a patch at lattice distance > 2
   /// (with wraps). Pure in the shape.
   [[nodiscard]] std::vector<CellId> passThroughCells(const PlaySurface& shaped, float gap);
   ```
   Use a uniform spatial hash with cell size = `gap`. The patches' 25 corners each, for
   all cells, are at most a few thousand points. Use `gap = 0.35`.
   - Lattice distance with wraps: use the variant's wrap sizes (grep how seams or the
     topology report sizes and wraps).
   - **Tests:**
     - empty for flat, cylinder, torus and Möbius at every `formed`;
     - non-empty for the fully formed Klein, and its cells form a band: each marked cell
       has ≥ 1 marked lattice neighbour;
     - deterministic order (sorted by CellId).
2. **Draw them as light.** For the cells in the set, while `skin` is on:
   - the tile patch is drawn with **hologram material (4)** instead of LED, using the
     hologram's opacity path (M24.3 uses `panelSignal[2] = −1` and colour alpha as
     opacity for material-4 instances; patches are vertices, not instances, so find how
     `surfaceVertices` choose a material; if there is no material per vertex, emit these
     cells into a **separate** vertex range drawn with the hologram pipeline/material,
     and note how);
   - the cell's colour is its normal fill (the same `fillFor(cell)`), with opacity 0.7;
   - **no membrane and no rib** there: the band is a gap in the hardware;
   - the cells must read as continuous with their LED neighbours: same footprint (not
     shrunk; light needs no tiles).
3. Add `PlaySurface::passThrough()` as a cached set, recomputed when `formed` changes by
   more than 0.002.

## Status

Steps 0/0b were committed). Implemented by Claude Opus 5.5 on user instruction.

What exists:
- Step 1: `CellStretch` / `cellStretch(shaped, flat)` in `src/render/play_surface.*`. The
  sampler is the private `PlaySurface::sampleAt`, shared by the patch loop, the seats and
  `pointAt`; the stretch reads it through `pointAt` (the 2-D shapes have no
  `playShapeAt`, which is the stacked path). The flat lengths are cached per variant and
  per sheet-shaping knob (`flatLengths`).
- Step 2: `SurfacePose::skin` (default off). With it, `PlaySurface::build` shrinks each
  tile by `min(1, 1/stretch)` per axis on top of `kCoverage`, and fills
  `membranePatches()`, `stretch()` and `passThrough()`. Picking is unchanged (the gapless
  `corners_`). `BoardOptions::slabSkin` is set by `main.cpp` (`optionsFor`) only on the lab
  game path with a surface; stacked shapes ignore it.
- Step 3: `src/render/slab_skin.*`: `slabRibs`, `appendRibs` (thin lab cylinders,
  metal, `#2A2E35`, Ø 0.012 m = 0.24 cells, inserted into the cylinder lab batch keeping
  the batches contiguous). `main.cpp`'s `skinRibs` joins them to the M24.5 yoke
  (`appendRig`'s `RigPiece::Kind::Yoke`). The slab back stays where it is.
- Step 4: `wrapCloseAt` (Tube {1, NaN}, Torus {`kTorusRollEnd`, 1}, Klein
  {`kKleinRingEnd`, `kKleinRingEnd`}, Band {1, NaN}: the seam rails' own stage table),
  `latchCrossed`, `latchBoost` (x2.5 / x1.6 / x1.2 / x1, calm none);
  `FxTimeline::latch` / `latchAge` (0.25 s, counted in `animating`). `main.cpp` stamps a
  latch when the live `geometryFormed` crosses a closing value upward, and `applyLatch`
  sets `BoardOptions::latchAxis` / `latchBoost`.
- Step 5: `passThroughCells` / `passThroughOf` (spatial hash, cell size `gap = 0.35`,
  lattice distance with wraps; a seam that reverses the other coordinate is crossed
  mirrored: Klein ranks, Moebius files). Band cells draw their full footprint as
  hologram light, no membrane, no ribs.
- Step 6: console `skin on|off`, `stretch`, `latch <axis>`; CLI `--skin 0|1` (default 1),
  `--latch-t <s>` and `--latch-axis 0|1` (default 1) for captures. `help()`,

- Step 7: `tests/render/test_slab_skin.cpp` (`[render][skin]`, 15 cases), the console case

Encodings and how things are drawn:
1. **Surface draw ranges.** The surface mesh has one material per draw (its identity
   instance), so `InstanceSet::surfaceDraws` splits it: LED tiles (the existing identity
   instance), the Klein light band (identity instance with material 4), the membrane
   (material 0.5) and, during a latch, the latch strip (material 5, blended). Empty with
   the skin off: then the old single draw runs.
2. **Light band:** hologram material 4 through the projector opacity path: vertex
   `panelSignal[2] = -1`, colour alpha 0.7 (the cell's own `fillFor` colour), drawn opaque
   with the screen door. Same footprint as its neighbours' tiles (coverage only, not
   shrunk). Its vertices keep the cell's fx record, so it powers down with the board.
3. **Membrane:** matte with the 0.5 sub-flag (matte's fraction was unused). Colour
   `#0B0F14`; the bridge glow strength `0.12 * smoothstep(1.02, 1.35, max(u, v))` rides
   the vertex heat channel, and `board.frag` adds `push.material.rgb` (the theme's ember,
   which is the manifold LED cyan `#35E0FF`; there is no `theme.ledOn`) times the
   serpentine `0.5 + 0.5 sin(40 u_cell)` (u_cell = the patch's file fraction,
   `fragPanel.y`) times the cell's power `fx.a.x * fx.a.y`. The code sits at the end of
   `main()` so no other fragment's shading changes.
4. **Latch flash:** a blended glow strip (material 5) a hair over the closing rail's
   faces, in the rail's own colour times the boost, alpha = the rail weight. Drawn only
   while a latch runs.

Deviations (each found by a test or a capture, not by taste):
1. **The membrane covers the whole cell footprint** (coverage 1), not "the current
   geometry" (coverage 0.9). At 0.9 the line between two cells was a slit, about 0.3 cells
   wide on a stretched torus cell, and the ribs inside showed through it as bars across
   the skin.
2. **Membrane power: the cell's fx record, not `fxCell = 0`.** Power is composed after
   the build (`applyBoardFx`), so the only way the membrane can go dark with the board is
   to read its cell's record; the matte path takes only power x flicker from it, so no LED
   effect touches it (the spec's intent).
3. **Torus stretch test:** the shipped torus's ring is about 2.2 times the flat board's
   length, so its inner equator is stretched too (v 1.27 inner, 3.10 outer); "< 0.9 on the
   inner" cannot hold without changing the morph maths, which this slice must not. The
   test asserts outer > 1.15 and inner < 0.5 x outer, and checks compression < 0.9 on the
   Moebius ribbon (v = 0.5). By eye the outer equator shows wide dark gaps and the inner
   much narrower ones, but not packed.
4. **Continuity test:** the Klein bottle's ring stage lengthens its ranks by about 0.1 per
   0.01 of `formed`. Cylinder, torus and Moebius hold < 0.05 per 0.01; the Klein is
   bounded by 0.11 per 0.01 and by 0.011 per 0.001 (Lipschitz, so no jump).
5. **Ribs:** one point per cell at the cell centres (not every half cell), and the even
   lattice lines `(k + 0.5) * size / 6` snapped to the nearest cell-centre line (on 8:
   0.5, 2.5, 3.5, 4.5, 6.5, 7.5). Points between seats sit off their tangent planes by the
   surface's sagitta, more than the 0.05-cell tolerance on the curled tube (0.65) and on
   stretched cells; two of the even lines fall exactly on cell boundaries. A rib across a
   seam that reverses the other coordinate (Moebius) stops at the seam instead of closing:
   past it the sheet's sides swap and the closing point lands on the playing side. A rib
   point or chord midpoint that is not at least 0.3 cells behind its nearest seat is left
   out (the rib splits there): the Klein figure-eight's lobes are thinner than the offset.
   The spokes necessarily pass through the skin (yoke outside, rib inside).
6. **`passThrough()` is computed in every skin build, not cached on a 0.002 `formed`
   tolerance.** A tolerance cache would make a frame depend on the previous one (not pure
   in explicit time); the hash test on 64 x 25 corners is cheap.
7. **The latch flash is a separate glow strip, not a boost of the rail weight.** The LED
   emitters normalise their own brightness (`kEmitterHdrCap / brightest`), so a x2.5 rail
   colour came out almost unchanged (about 35 pixels at 960 x 540). Boosting through a
   shader channel instead changed lavapipe's code generation for the whole LED branch and
   broke byte identity of lab-off frames by sub-LSB noise; the strip needs no shader
   change.
8. `main.cpp` was taken through `clang-format` whole (a few pre-existing unformatted lines
   in it changed), since precommit checks every staged file whole.
