## What this is, in one paragraph

Today, with the lab on, the menus live in the lab, but starting a game cuts to the old
dark board scene: no lab, no physical continuity. After this slice, a game **happens in
the lab**:
- the board is a physical slab hanging from the ceiling gantry over the open floor;
- starting a game makes the machine fetch the slab from the rack and bring it there,
  while the camera flies from the bench to the board;
- the board boots once it is clamped;
- leaving the game powers the board down, and the slab goes back to the rack;
- during play, the room dims ("focus mode") so the board reads as before;
- walls between the eye and the board fade away, and the orbit cannot leave the room.

## Design decisions fixed by this spec (do not revisit)

1. **The board is the frame of reference.** All existing board code works in **board
   space**: picking (`view::pickBox` on `Session` placements), fx, seams, move
   animation, morphs, the game camera and its framing (`OrbitCamera::frame`,
   `easeMorphFraming`, follow and cinema). It must not change. So, during a game, the
   **lab is placed around the board**, not the board into the lab:
   - `RigXform` maps lab world (cells, `LabScene::toWorld`) to board space;
   - lab instances, lab lights and station cameras go through it;
   - board instances and the game camera are untouched, so picking needs no change.
2. **Scale.** One cell is 5 cm in both spaces (`kCellsPerMetre = 20`), so for a layout
   that fits, the scale is 1. The deployment volume is in Step 1. If a layout's bounds
   do not fit, the lab is scaled up around it: `labScale = max(1, needed)` in board
   units. In the player's eyes, the room grows rather than the board shrinking, which
   is the same picture as `rigScale < 1` in the overview. M24.6 refines this with
   quarter tiles; M24.5 only needs the uniform fit.
3. **Hanging.** A slab hangs from a mast at its **far edge** (board +Y, the side away
   from the default camera). A mast in the middle of the board would cross the playing
   surface. At the mast's foot, a horizontal **yoke** runs under the slab to the back's
   connector boss (`slab_back.obj`, Ø 0.07 m at the centre). Slices that share the same
   mast position (a vertical stack, as in cube5) share one mast, with one yoke per
   slice.
4. **During travel, a stand-in slab moves, not the board.** While a slab is travelling, a
   plain dark slab (lab mesh `slab`, the same part type as `magazine.slab.*`) is drawn
   in its place. At the moment it clamps (deploy) or unclamps (stow), the real board
   (dark, powered off) replaces the stand-in, or the reverse. Both are dark at that
   moment, so the swap is invisible: check this by eye in a 2× crop.
5. **Up to 4 mast groups travel.** A layout whose slices group into at most 4 mast
   positions gets the full animated deploy and stow. Larger layouts deploy
   **statically**: everything appears in place, with masts, and the camera still flies.
   M24.6 makes them travel.

### 2.1 RigXform

```cpp
/// Lab world (cells, LabScene::toWorld) -> board space, during a game (M24.5).
struct RigXform {
  Vec3 labOrigin{};     ///< board-space position of the lab-world origin (gantry.centre)
  float labScale{1};    ///< lab units per board unit; >= 1
  [[nodiscard]] Vec3 toBoard(const Vec3& labWorld) const noexcept;   // labOrigin + w*labScale
  [[nodiscard]] Vec3 toLab(const Vec3& board) const noexcept;        // inverse
};
/// Place a layout: its bounds' centre (x, y) and its bounds' **bottom** z sit at the
/// deploy anchor; the lab scales up only if the bounds (plus a 0.08 m margin per side,
/// in cells) exceed the deployment volume.
[[nodiscard]] RigXform placeLayout(const Bounds& layout, const DeployVolume& volume) noexcept;
```
`DeployVolume` mirrors the `[deploy]` table, in lab-world cells; convert it with
`toWorld`. The anchor height is `gantry.centre`.

**Tests:**
- standard (8×8, bounds ±4): `labScale == 1`, and the bounds' centre maps to the lab
  origin;
- `toLab(toBoard(p)) == p` within 1e-5;
- a layout 3× wider than the volume gives `labScale` ≈ the ratio including margins;
- the bounds' bottom maps to the anchor's z.

### 2.2 Mast groups

```cpp
struct SlabSpec {           ///< one slice's physical slab, in board space
  std::uint32_t slice{0};
  float minX{0}, maxX{0}, minY{0}, maxY{0};  ///< bezel extents (cells + bezel margin)
  float z{0};               ///< the slice's face height
};
struct MastGroup {
  Vec3 mastFoot{};          ///< board space: lowest yoke attachment on the mast axis
  float mastTopZ{0};        ///< board space: the carriage underside (rail_z mapped)
  std::vector<std::uint32_t> slabs;  ///< indices into the SlabSpec list, top to bottom
};
[[nodiscard]] std::vector<SlabSpec> slabsOf(const std::vector<Placement>& placements,
                                            float bezelMargin);
[[nodiscard]] std::vector<MastGroup> mastGroups(const std::vector<SlabSpec>& slabs,
                                                const RigXform& x, const DeployInfo& d);
```
- `slabsOf` groups placements by `slice`. Extents are min/max of the cell centres
  ± (0.5 + `bezelMargin`). Use the margin the renderer uses for the bezel; grep it in
  `board_renderer.cpp`, and if it is not a single named constant, make it one.
- A slab's mast axis: x = slab centre x, y = `maxY + mast_edge_gap` (converted to
  cells, divided by `labScale`). Slabs whose axes are within 0.5 cells of each other
  share a group.
- **Tests:**
  - standard: 1 slab and 1 group, with the mast at y = 4 + bezel + gap;
  - cube5: 5 slabs and 1 group with 5 slabs, top to bottom;
  - a 2×2 grid of slices: 4 groups;
  - groups are ordered by slice index of their first slab, so the result is
    deterministic.

### 2.3 The slab path

```cpp
struct SlabPathSpec {
  Vec3 bayCentre{};        ///< board space: the parked stand-in's centre in the magazine
  std::array<float,4> bayQuat{};   ///< its orientation (edge-on, rot [90,90,0])
  Vec3 pickCentre{};       ///< where the carriage takes it: bay slid out to x = rail limit
  Vec3 hangCentre{};       ///< the deployed slab centre (from SlabSpec + RigXform)
  float departure{0};      ///< seconds after the move starts that this slab leaves
};
struct SlabPose {
  Vec3 centre{};
  std::array<float,4> quat{};   ///< slab orientation
  Vec3 carriage{};              ///< carriage underside centre (x, y; z = rail)
  float mastLength{0};          ///< from the carriage to the yoke
  float clampPulse{0};          ///< 0..1, the short glow when it locks
  bool clamped{false};          ///< true from the lock on (deploy)
  bool inBay{true};
};
/// Deploy pose at `t` seconds after the move start. Stow is the same path reversed
/// in time with its own departure order (Step 4).
[[nodiscard]] SlabPose slabDeploy(const SlabPathSpec& s, float t, float intensity) noexcept;
```
Phases, in seconds after `departure`:

| Phase | Time | What |
|---|---|---|
| slide | 0 – 0.30 | the stand-in slides out of its slot along −X to `pickCentre`; rigEase |
| tilt and lift | 0.30 – 0.55 | it rotates from edge-on to face-up (slerp `bayQuat` → identity) while the carriage takes it; the mast shortens to hold it just under the rail |
| travel | 0.55 – 1.05 | the carriage runs along the rails to above `hangCentre` (x first and y together; straight line); rigEase over the distance |
| lower | 1.05 – 1.30 | the mast extends; the slab descends to `hangCentre` |
| settle | 1.30 – 1.42 | a damped overshoot of 2.5 mm (0.05 cells) downwards and back: `−0.05·exp(−k·u)·sin(π·u·2)` for u in 0..1 |
| clamp | 1.42 | `clamped = true`; `clampPulse` rises to 1 in 0.03 s and falls to 0 by 0.25 s |

- **Departures** overlap: slab group i departs at `0.2 + 0.12·i` s; slabs within one group
  depart together (they are on one mast, so they are carried as a stack). The last lock
  is therefore at `0.2 + 0.12·(groups − 1) + 1.42` ≤ 1.98 s for 4 groups.
- **Calm intensity:** `slabDeploy` returns the final pose for every t ≥ 0
  (`clamped = true`, `clampPulse = 0`); the move is the cross-fade.
- **Tests:**
  - at t < departure, the pose is the bay pose and `inBay`;
  - at t = departure + 1.42 + ε, the centre equals `hangCentre` within 1e-4 and
    `clamped`;
  - the overshoot never exceeds 0.05 cells and ends at 0;
  - the centre is continuous: no jump > 0.6 cells between 1 ms steps;
  - the mast length is ≥ 0 and the carriage stays inside the rails' x/y range;
  - the quaternion is unit length throughout;
  - calm returns the final pose.

## Step 3 — drawing the lab during a game

In `main.cpp`'s frame build (the block that ends with `labBackdrop(...)`):

1. **New condition** `labGame = labEnabled(shell) && shell.showsBoard() && !Editor`. It
   is on the lab path whenever a game screen or pause is shown with the lab on. Keep
   `labBackdrop` for menus. While `labGame`:
   - build the board instances exactly as today (unchanged);
   - compute `RigXform` from the session's placements (`boundsOf`) and the `[deploy]`
     volume. Cache it per (session id, layout key), recomputing when the layout changes
     (M24.6 animates that change; here it may snap);
   - append the lab with a transform: add an overload
     `LabScene::appendTo(InstanceSet&, theme, salience, const view::RigXform&)`. Each lab
     instance's centre goes through `toBoard`, and its scale is multiplied by
     `labScale`. The default (no xform) is today's call, unchanged;
   - append the **rig hardware** (Step 3.2);
   - do **not** set the camera from a station: the camera stays `boardCamera(...)`,
     clamped (Step 3.4).
2. **Rig hardware per mast group**, from `mastGroups` and the current `SlabPose`s:
   - a carriage plus its two bridges (`appendCopy` of the templates at the group's
     carriage x/y). The bridges span the full Y rail length and move with the carriage
     in x;
   - the mast: `gantry_mast_outer` fixed under the carriage, and `gantry_mast_inner`
     sliding out of it to reach `mastLength`. If the reach exceeds the two tubes' 1.15
     m, scale both tubes' z by `reach / 1.15`, keeping the collar rings at their ends.
     Note it;
   - per slab: one **yoke**, a `bevel` box 0.03 × 0.03 m in section, from the mast axis
     to the slab's centre under its back, at `yoke_drop`, matte dark metal; plus the
     **slab back** `slab_back.obj` scaled in x/y to the slab's extents (z unchanged),
     top at the slab's underside;
   - while a slab is travelling (not clamped, during deploy or stow): the **stand-in**
     slab instead of the real board for that slice. Hide that slice's board instances
     (they are built but skipped). Find where instances carry their slice, or filter
     by `fxCell` → cell → placement slice. Prefer a per-slice visibility mask passed to
     `buildInstances` if one exists (grep `boardVisible`): the session's
     `boardVisible(c)` lambda is already the hook; AND it with "this cell's slice is
     clamped".
   - `clampPulse` lights the inner mast's clamp ring (glow material, the LED cyan, alpha
     = pulse) for 0.25 s;
   - the parked stand-ins in the magazine (`magazine.slab.0..7`): those currently out on
     a deploy are not drawn in the bay.
3. **Lights during a game** (`labLightBlock`):
   - raise the light cap from 8 to **12**: the std140 array size in `GpuLightBlock`, the
     shader's loop bound and its `static_assert`s. Lab-off output must stay
     byte-identical: the light loop only runs on the lab path;
   - **focus mode:** the `room` group's intensity is multiplied by `focus`, which eases
     from 1 to **0.15** over 1.0 s after the deploy's last lock and back to 1 during
     stow. Add a `float roomGroupScale` parameter. `machine` lights (projector, magazine)
     are unchanged;
   - **slab glow:** one point light per mast group, at most **2** (merge the rest into
     the nearest by summing intensity). Position: 0.12 m above the group's top slab
     centre. Colour: the mean emitted colour of that group's cell instances (average
     `color.rgb × gain` over cells with power > 0; grep how the fx sets
     power/brightness). Intensity: 0.6 × mean power, so a powered-down board darkens
     the room;
   - transform light positions with `RigXform::toBoard` and radii with `labScale`.
4. **Camera during a game:**
   - the orbit distance is clamped with `clampOrbitDistance(camera, d, roomBounds, 0.15 m
     in cells)`. Apply it where the camera is finalised each frame (after
     `boardCamera`). Also clamp the user's zoom input in the same place, so the stored
     distance never drifts outside: grep where scroll-zoom writes the camera's
     distance;
   - **wall fade:** for each room part (group `room`, and shelves, boxes and the bench),
     set its fade with `wallFade(partBoundsInBoard, eye, target, 0.4 m)`. The
     screen-door discard in `board.frag` is a 4×4 Bayer threshold against the fade.
     **Instance field:** check whether `Instance` has a spare lane on the lab path. A
     field documented as unused for lab parts is acceptable; `panelSignal[2]` is taken
     by salience. If there is none, add `float fade{1}`, update the vertex input and
     every `static_assert` on the size, and note it.

## Step 4 — the moves

1. **Classification.** Following M24.4a's upgrade of `Cut` to `Station`, upgrade:
   - `MenuToGame` → `Deploy`, when the lab path is on (menu station → game);
   - `GameToMenu` → `Stow`, when the lab path is on.
   Add both to `ScreenMove`, `moveSeconds` and `moveFx`, with tests in
   `tests/unit/app/test_screen_transition.cpp`.
2. **Deploy timeline** (t from the move start; total `T = max(3.0, lastLock + 1.0)`):
   - 0.0 – 0.3: the implant panel dissolves (M24.4b record, leaving) and the bench
     hologram collapses into its lens (M24.4a collapse);
   - 0.2 – lastLock: slabs travel (`slabDeploy` per group);
   - 0.4 – lastLock: the camera flies from the menu station (converted to board space with
     `RigXform`; this is the lab's camera, already in lab world) to the **game camera** for
     this game (`boardCamera(...)` at its framed pose). Use `view::blendCamera` with
     `rigEase`. Through 0.4 – 1.0, aim the blend's target at the first slab's live
     centre and then at the layout centre: blend the target point between those two by
     `smoothstep(0.4, 1.4, t)`, so the eye follows the slab;
   - lastLock: **the boot starts.** The FxTimeline stamps `bootStart_ = now` when it
     sees a new `bootSerial` (`fx_timeline.cpp` around line 27). Add a way to delay it:
     `FxTimeline::holdBootUntil(double visualSeconds)` stores a gate; the boot stamps
     at `max(now, gate)`. `main.cpp` sets the gate to `moveStart + lastLock` when a
     Deploy starts;
   - lastLock – T: focus mode eases in (Step 3.3); the HUD forms as implant panels (M24.4b
     records for the HUD panes; if the HUD panes are not yet routed through
     `beginScreenPane`, record them the same way and note it).
   - **Input:** none until lastLock, except skip. **Skip** (any key or click,
     `Ui::skipTransition`) jumps to the final state with the boot just started.
3. **Stow timeline** (game → menu, total about 2.8 s plus the shutdown when live):
   - if the game is live: M23.12 §2's **compressed** shutdown over 1.0 s
     (`compressedShutdown = true`, `holdProgress` 0 → 1), with its 3 s delay skipped. If
     the game had already ended and shut down, skip this (0 s);
   - then, per group in **reverse** order, the slab path **reversed in time**
     (`slabDeploy(spec, P − u)`, where u runs from the group's own stow departure and P is
     the path's full length 1.42 s). Departures are at `0.12·i` after the shutdown;
   - the camera flies back to the `menu` station over the same span; the room focus eases
     back to 1;
   - at the end: the bench hologram grows out of its lens (M24.4a grow), and the main
     menu forms (M24.4b record, arriving).
4. **Game end** is unchanged: slabs stay deployed, dark, with their standby LEDs.
   **Rematch** reboots in place: no slab movement and no move.
5. **Calm intensity:** both moves are the 0.3 s cross-fade between the two camera poses.
   Slabs are already in place (deploy) or already parked (stow); the focus level
   switches at the end.
6. **Static layouts** (more than 4 groups; design decision 5): no slab travel. Slabs,
   masts and carriages are at their final poses from t = 0, and stand-ins are not used.
   The camera still flies, and the boot still waits for 1.4 s, so the rhythm matches.
