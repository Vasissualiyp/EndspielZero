## §1 Seam-legend hover

### What the player sees

The variant plate (top-left HUD panel) lists one swatch per glued drawn axis, for
example `■ file edges  ■ rank edges` on a torus. When the mouse is over a swatch or its
label:
- every seam on that axis brightens noticeably and blooms a little under post;
- every other glued seam dims to about half;
- mirrors (silver rails, `mirrorbox`) never change.

Moving the mouse away restores everything on the next frame. There is no easing: a hover
answers the hand at once.

### Background you need

- Seams are described by `view::SeamFace` in `src/view/seams.hpp`. Each face has
  `screenAxis` (0 or 1: which drawn axis the face is on), `kind` (`Glued` or `Mirror`)
  and `color`.
- `view::seamLegend(seams, cfg, dims)` in `src/view/seams.cpp` (around line 143) turns
  faces into `std::vector<SeamLegendEntry>`, one per axis name, skipping mirrors. Today
  `SeamLegendEntry` has `axis` (the name) and `color` only.
- The legend is drawn in `src/render/ui.cpp`. Search for the comment
  `// Name the seam colours`. Each entry draws a filled rect, an `ImGui::Dummy`, then the
  label text `e.axis + " edges"`.
- Seams reach the board in three places in `src/render/board_renderer.cpp`:
  1. **Flat rails:** search for `// A seam is drawn as a coloured rail along the face`.
     One `Instance rail` per `SeamFace` is pushed to `byShape[Archetype::Cell]`, with
     `toFloat4(f.color, rail.color)`.
  2. **Surface rails** (geometry view): search for `SurfaceRail ru = surfaceRail(*seams,
     patch.cell, 0, i, j);`. `ru` is the axis-0 rail and `rv` the axis-1 rail; their
     `.color` is mixed into the patch vertex colour.
  3. There is no third place. The plinth `edge` colour is not a seam colour. Do not
     touch `plinthInstance`.
- Board options are `render::BoardOptions` in `src/render/board_renderer.hpp`
  (`struct BoardOptions`, around line 93). The front end builds them in
  `optionsFor(const app::Shell&)` in `src/gui/main.cpp` (around line 402).

## §2 The holograms shut down

### What is wrong now

When a game ends, three things happen in order:
1. The board holds the final position for `kEndDelaySeconds` (3 s).
2. The LEDs power down in a wave from the losing royal.
3. The loser's holograms destabilise to 65 % opacity with a shimmer, and the winner's
   gain 15 % emission.

Then nothing more happens, ever: the pieces stay lit on a dark board. The pieces are
projections from the slab, so a slab losing power must lose its projections too. The
board should end fully dark, apart from one small standby light.

### What the player sees (decisive end)

All ages are seconds since the result was decided, the same clock `--end-t` uses.
1. **0 – 3 s:** the final position is held at full power. Unchanged.
2. **From 3 s:** the LED wave runs from the losing royal at 3 cells per second, each
   cell with a 0.6 s glitch and a 1.5 s fall to the hold level. Unchanged
   (`view::powerDown`). The loser's holograms destabilise as the front passes them, as
   now (`view::hologramEnd`).
3. **After the wave:** the pieces' projectors fail one at a time, in this order:
   1. the loser's non-royal pieces, **farthest from the origin first**, so the loser's
      army collapses inward toward its fallen king;
   2. the loser's royal (the mated king, which until now keeps full light);
   3. the winner's non-royal pieces, **nearest to the origin first**;
   4. then a pause of 0.8 s;
   5. the winner's royal, last. During the pause it stands alone on the dead board.
4. **Each piece's failure (0.7 s):** a hologram losing its beam, not a fade.
   - **0 – 0.15 s:** two or three hard dropouts (opacity to 0.15 for 30–50 ms each) with
     the scan jitter at maximum.
   - **0.15 – 0.55 s:** the projection collapses downward into its base. The visible
     height falls from the top to nothing, accelerating like something falling. A
     bright scan band rides the falling top edge; the shader already draws it for the
     boot's reveal.
   - **0.55 – 0.70 s:** the cell's projector base flashes warm (heat 1.0) and decays to
     nothing. That is the projector's last light, and it blooms under post.
5. **When the last piece has gone:** the slab is dark at its hold level, with no
   holograms. One small standby LED glows on the bezel corner nearest the winner, pulsing
   slowly (0.5 Hz) in the winner's colour.

### Draw

- The LED dim is unchanged (`powerDown` with `EndKind::Draw`).
- The shutdown starts after the dim (`kEndDelaySeconds + kDrawDimSeconds`).
- Both armies are interleaved, ordered by distance from the **board centre cell**,
  outermost first. Ties go white before black, then lower cell id first.
- Both royals go last, together (same start time), with no pause before them.
- The standby LED is the theme's `bone` colour and pulses at 0.25 Hz.

### Calm (presentation intensity below 0.5)

- No dropouts, no collapse and no base flash. Each piece fades its opacity from its
  current value to 0 over 0.6 s.
- Same order, but every piece starts 0.05 s after the previous one, with no king pause.
- The standby LED is steady, not pulsing.

### Undo out of the end

The existing power-up (`view::powerUp`, `view::hologramRestore`, `kPowerUpSeconds` =
0.5 s) runs when an undo takes a finished game back. It must also bring back shut-down
pieces:
- `reveal` goes 0 → 1 over 0.35 s for every piece at once, with one dropout
  (opacity 0.3, 40 ms, at a seeded time between 0.05 and 0.15 s);
- the standby LED is gone from the first frame of the power-up.

### Review

- Entering review shows the reviewed position fully lit, with every piece visible. A
  reviewed position must be readable.
- Leaving review returns to the end state at its current age: dark and shut down,
  immediately, with no replay.
- Implementation: in the front end, mask the end effect while `session.reviewPly()` has a
  value (see Step 5). The game and `FxTimeline` keep counting, so leaving review lands on
  the right age.

### Game → menu while a game is live

`applyMoveHold` in `src/gui/main.cpp` compresses the decisive power-down into the 0.8 s
screen move (M23.11 §6). During that hold the pieces must also go out: every piece
collapses at the same time. The collapse starts at 40 % of the hold's progress and is
complete at 95 %, using the decisive collapse curve squeezed into that window. There is
no ordering, no king pause and no standby LED. See Step 4.

### Step 1 — the pure model (test)

Add to `src/view/game_end_fx.hpp`, below `hologramRestore`:

```cpp
/// The projectors failing once the panel has powered down (M23.12 section 2).
inline constexpr float kShutdownStagger = 0.12f;  ///< between one piece's start and the next
inline constexpr float kShutdownSpread = 3.0f;    ///< the whole army, at most
inline constexpr float kKingPause = 0.8f;         ///< before the winning royal, decisive only
inline constexpr float kCollapseSeconds = 0.7f;   ///< one piece's failure
inline constexpr float kCalmShutdownStagger = 0.05f;
inline constexpr float kCalmShutdownFade = 0.6f;
inline constexpr float kRestoreRevealSeconds = 0.35f;

/// One standing piece, as the shutdown order needs it.
struct ShutdownPiece {
  Color side{Color::White};
  bool royal{false};
  int dist{0};             ///< from the end's origin (decisive) or the board centre (draw)
  std::uint32_t cell{0};   ///< tie-break only
};

/// When each piece's projector starts to fail, in seconds since the decision; parallel
/// to `pieces`. `firstStart` is when the first one may start (see `shutdownBegins`).
[[nodiscard]] std::vector<float> shutdownStarts(const EndFx& end,
                                                std::span<const ShutdownPiece> pieces,
                                                float firstStart, float intensity);

/// The earliest a projector may fail: after the LED wave has passed the farthest cell
/// and that cell's glitch is over (decisive), or after the dim (draw). `maxDist` is
/// the largest distance from the origin over the board.
[[nodiscard]] float shutdownBegins(EndKind kind, int maxDist, float intensity) noexcept;

/// One piece's hologram `age` seconds after its own start. Before 0: untouched (all
/// fields neutral). After kCollapseSeconds (calm: kCalmShutdownFade): gone.
struct HologramShutdown {
  float opacity{1};     ///< multiplies the hologram's opacity
  float reveal{1};      ///< visible height 0..1; >= 1 = whole
  float scanJitter{0};  ///< added to the existing jitter, clamp the sum to 1
  float baseHeat{0};    ///< the projector's last flash, written to CellFx::heat
  [[nodiscard]] bool gone() const noexcept { return opacity <= 0.0f || reveal <= 0.0f; }
};
[[nodiscard]] HologramShutdown hologramShutdown(float age, std::uint32_t seed,
                                                float intensity);

/// The same piece brought back by an undo: `age` seconds into the power-up.
[[nodiscard]] HologramShutdown hologramUnshutdown(float age, std::uint32_t seed,
                                                  float intensity);

/// When the standby LED appears: the last start plus one failure.
[[nodiscard]] float shutdownComplete(std::span<const float> starts, float intensity) noexcept;

/// The standby LED's brightness 0..1 at `age` seconds since the decision.
/// 0 before `complete`. Decisive: 0.5 Hz, 0.35..1. Draw: 0.25 Hz, 0.35..1. Calm: 0.8.
[[nodiscard]] float standbyLevel(EndKind kind, float age, float complete,
                                 float intensity) noexcept;
```

Add `#include <span>` and `#include <vector>` to the header.

Implement them in `src/view/game_end_fx.cpp`, reusing the file's `smooth` and `unit`
helpers.

**`shutdownBegins`:**
```
calm (intensity < 0.5):  kEndDelaySeconds + kCalmFadeSeconds
draw:                    kEndDelaySeconds + kDrawDimSeconds
decisive:                kEndDelaySeconds + maxDist / kPowerDownCellsPerSecond
                         + kPowerDownGlitchSeconds
```

**`shutdownStarts`:**
1. Build an index list and sort it into firing order:
   - Decisive: group 0 is loser non-royal, sorted by `dist` descending; group 1 is
     loser royal; group 2 is winner non-royal, sorted by `dist` ascending; group 3 is
     winner royal.
   - Draw: non-royals sorted by `dist` descending, ties white first, then lower `cell`;
     then all royals.
   - All ties: lower `cell` first. The order must be total, so the result is
     deterministic.
2. Stagger: `s = intensity < 0.5 ? kCalmShutdownStagger : kShutdownStagger`. If
   `(n_nonfinal - 1) * s > kShutdownSpread`, set `s = kShutdownSpread / (n_nonfinal - 1)`.
   `n_nonfinal` is the number of pieces before the final group (the winner's royal for
   decisive, the royals for draw).
3. Assign `firstStart + k * s` to the k-th piece in order, for the non-final pieces.
4. The final group:
   - Decisive at full intensity: `last_nonfinal_start + s + kKingPause`.
   - Draw, or calm: `last_nonfinal_start + s`, the same value for every royal.
   - If there are no non-final pieces, the final group starts at `firstStart`.
5. A variant with no royal pieces simply has no final group.
6. A decisive end where the loser has no pieces left skips groups 0 and 1.

**`hologramShutdown(age, seed, intensity)`:**
```
age < 0                      -> neutral {1,1,0,0}
calm:  opacity = 1 - smooth(age / kCalmShutdownFade); reveal 1; jitter 0; heat 0
       (opacity reaches exactly 0 at age >= kCalmShutdownFade)
full:
  0.00 .. 0.15  dropouts: n = 2 + (seed % 2); dropout i starts at
                0.01 + 0.13 * unit(seed ^ (0x51ED + i*0x9E37)) and lasts
                0.03 + 0.02 * unit(seed ^ (0xD1CE + i*0x7F4A)); inside one, opacity 0.15.
                scanJitter 1.
  0.15 .. 0.55  reveal = 1 - x*x, where x = (age - 0.15) / 0.40
                (ease-in: slow then fast, "falling"); opacity 1 (outside dropouts);
                scanJitter 1.
  0.55 .. 0.70  reveal 0; baseHeat = 1.0 * (1 - (age - 0.55) / 0.15)
  >= 0.70       reveal 0, opacity 0, baseHeat 0
```
Clamp every output to its range.

**`hologramUnshutdown(age, seed, intensity)`:**
```
age < 0                -> {opacity 1, reveal 0, jitter 0, heat 0}   (still gone)
calm:  reveal 1; opacity = smooth(age / kRestoreRevealSeconds)
full:  reveal = smooth(age / kRestoreRevealSeconds); opacity 1, except one dropout of
       0.04 s starting at 0.05 + 0.10 * unit(seed ^ 0xB007) where opacity = 0.3;
       scanJitter = 1 - smooth(age / kRestoreRevealSeconds)
age >= kRestoreRevealSeconds -> neutral
```

**`shutdownComplete`:** `max(starts) + (calm ? kCalmShutdownFade : kCollapseSeconds)`.
For empty `starts`, return the `firstStart` the caller used. Since `firstStart` is not
an argument, the caller handles the empty case; document that in the header comment.

**`standbyLevel`:** `age < complete` → 0. Calm → 0.8. Otherwise
`0.35 + 0.65 * (0.5 + 0.5 * sin(2π f (age - complete) - π/2))`, with `f` = 0.5 for
decisive and 0.25 for draw. The `-π/2` makes it start from its dim end, so the LED fades
in rather than popping on at full.

### Pitfalls

- `CellFx::reveal` is read by the shader only for pieces (`board.frag`:
  `if (isPiece && fx.a.z < 1.0 && fragHeight >= fx.a.z) discard;`). A reveal of 0 discards
  the whole piece. That is the intended "gone".
- The base heat is `fx.a.w`. The shader does
  `heat = max(heat * smoothstep(0.0, 0.25, fx.a.z), fx.a.w)`. While reveal is 0, the
  standing footprint heat is multiplied away, and your `baseHeat` is what remains. That
  is exactly the flash. Check that a heat of 1.0 is not clamped lower anywhere in the
  shader (grep `heat` in `board.frag`). If it is, keep 1.0 and note the clamp.
- One `CellFx` record per cell carries both the cell's and its piece's fields. Do not
  write piece-only fields on empty cells.
- `FxTimeline::end` has no end time: a finished game keeps its `endAge` growing forever.
  All new functions must be stable for large ages, such as 3600 s. Add one test at
  `endAge = 3600`.
- The trailer (`shell.attracting()`) must never show a shutdown. Check that the trailer
  does not produce an `endAge` (grep `attracting` near the fx code). If it can, gate the
  shutdown on `!attracting`.
