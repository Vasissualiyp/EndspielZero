## §1 Seam-legend hover

### What

When the pointer is over a seam swatch, or its label, in the variant plate (`Ui`'s legend
loop, `src/render/ui.cpp`, the `seamKey` block), that axis's seams on the board brighten.
Every other seam dims slightly, so the hovered one stands out even on a busy Klein bottle.
Moving the pointer off restores everything.

### How

- The legend entry needs to know its drawn axis. Add `std::uint8_t screenAxis` to
  `view::SeamLegendEntry` (`src/view/seams.hpp`), filled by `seamLegend` from the faces it
  groups.
- `Ui` records the hovered entry each frame: `std::optional<std::uint8_t> seamHover_`.
  It is set when `ImGui::IsItemHovered()` is true for either the swatch dummy or the label
  (wrap both in a group). It is exposed as `Ui::seamHover()`.
- Carry it to the renderer as a presentation option, not a setting:
  `BoardOptions::seamHighlightAxis` (`int`, `-1` = none). `optionsFor` in
  `src/gui/main.cpp` sets it from `ui->seamHover()`. There is a one-frame lag, which is
  fine.
- `BoardRenderer` multiplies the seam colour where it is written:
  - flat rails, the per-face instances near `board_renderer.cpp`'s "A seam is drawn as a
    coloured rail" block: `face.screenAxis == axis` gets ×`kSeamHoverGain`, other glued
    faces ×`kSeamHoverDim`;
  - surface rails (`surfaceRail` calls for axes 0 and 1): the same rule on the rail's
    axis;
  - edge-mask colours on cells (`Instance::edge`): the same rule.

  Mirrors (silver) are never changed.
- Constants, in a header next to the seam ramp: `kSeamHoverGain = 1.8` (on the colour,
  clamped so it stays in HDR range and blooms a little); `kSeamHoverDim = 0.55`.
- Easing: none. The hover changes immediately, which is what a hover should do. No
  intensity scaling is needed, because a hover is user input, not an effect.
- Picking is untouched: no geometry changes, only colour.

### Debug access

- Console: `seamhover <axis>|off`. This pins the highlight to a legend axis by name
  (`file`, `rank`, ...) until `off`, overriding the pointer. It lets someone check the
  effect without a mouse.
- CLI: `--seam-hover <axis>` for captures.

### Tests

- Unit: `seamLegend` reports the right `screenAxis` for cylinder (one entry), torus and
  Klein (two entries).
- GPU (`[render]`): build instances for `klein` with `seamHighlightAxis = 0`. Every
  axis-0 glued face's edge colour is brighter than with `-1`, every axis-1 glued face is
  dimmer, mirrors (`mirrorbox`) are unchanged, and the instance count and positions are
  identical (picking-neutral).
- Capture: `klein` with `--seam-hover file` and `--seam-hover rank`, in both themes, on
  the flat board and with `--geometry --formed 1`.

## §2 The holograms shut down

### What is wrong now

After the 3 s hold, the LEDs power down in a wave from the losing royal. The loser's
holograms only destabilise (opacity 0.65 with a shimmer), the winner's gain 15 %, and
then everything stays standing on a dark board indefinitely. A dark slab with lit pieces
on it is not "switched off". The pieces are projections from the slab
(`visual_ideas.md`, approved direction), so when the slab's power fails, its projectors
fail too.

### The new sequence (decisive end)

All ages are seconds since the decision, as `--end-t` measures them.

1. **0 – 3 s:** the final position is held at full power. Unchanged.
2. **LED wave:** unchanged (`view::powerDown`, 3 cells/s from the origin, a 0.6 s glitch,
   a 1.5 s fall to the hold level). The loser's holograms destabilise as the front
   passes them, as now (`hologramEnd`).
3. **Shutdown order.** Once the LED front has passed every cell (its last arrival is
   `kEndDelaySeconds + maxDist / kPowerDownCellsPerSecond`, plus `kPowerDownGlitchSeconds`),
   the projectors shut down one piece at a time:
   - the loser's pieces first, farthest from the origin first, so the loser's army
     collapses inward toward its fallen king;
   - then the loser's royal, the mated king, which until now keeps its full light (the
     existing `mated` rule);
   - then the winner's pieces, nearest to the origin first;
   - the winner's royal last.

   One piece starts every `kShutdownStagger = 0.12 s`. The stagger is capped so the
   whole army takes at most `kShutdownSpread = 3.0 s`; with more pieces than 25, the
   stagger shrinks to fit. After the winner's second-to-last piece starts, there is an
   extra pause of `kKingPause = 0.8 s` before the winning royal starts. That pause is the
   moment the winner stands alone on the dead board.
4. **One piece shutting down** (`kCollapseSeconds = 0.7 s`). This is the hologram losing
   its beam, not a fade:
   - **0 – 0.15:** 2 to 3 hard dropouts (opacity to 0.15 for 30–50 ms, seeded per cell)
     with scan jitter at its maximum.
   - **0.15 – 0.55:** the projection collapses downward into its base. Use the existing
     `CellFx::reveal`, which the boot already uses to build a piece upward, running
     1 → 0. Use an ease-in, so the piece accelerates down as if falling into the emitter.
     The scan lines thicken (scan dominance → 1), so the last of it is pure scan.
   - **0.55 – 0.7:** a bright pinpoint at the base, `CellFx::heat` spiked to 1.5 then
     decaying to 0. It is the projector's last light, and it blooms under post. Then
     nothing.
5. **Standby.** When the last piece has gone, the slab is dark at its hold level with no
   holograms. One physical cue stays on: a small standby LED on the bezel corner nearest
   the winner, pulsing at 0.5 Hz in the winner's colour. It is drawn as a glow instance on
   the bezel (`matId 5`). It replaces nothing; the board was never this dark before. With
   M24, this LED is part of the slab hardware.

### Draw

The LED dim is unchanged. Then both armies shut down together, interleaved: a piece of
one colour, then the other, ordered by distance from the board centre, outermost first.
There is no king pause and both royals go last together. The standby LED is neutral
bone and pulses at 0.25 Hz.

### Calm (intensity below one half)

No dropouts and no pinpoint. Every piece fades out over 0.6 s, in the same order but
with a 0.05 s stagger and no king pause. It still communicates the end; it just doesn't
perform it.

### Undo out of the end

The existing power-up (`powerUp`, `hologramRestore`) must bring shut-down pieces back.
Add a reverse of the collapse: `reveal` 0 → 1 over 0.35 s with one dropout, all pieces
together (the boot builds pieces the same way). The standby LED goes off at once.

### The result panel and the review

- The result panel is unaffected. It appears where it does now.
- **Review** shows pieces: entering review (`review <ply>`) cancels the shutdown for the
  reviewed position. The board powers up exactly as an undo does (§2 "Undo"), because a
  reviewed position must be readable. `review off` returns to the final position and
  shows it shut down again, at once, with no replay.
- **Rematch** already boots the board; nothing new.

### Code

- `src/view/game_end_fx.{hpp,cpp}`: add, pure,
  ```cpp
  inline constexpr float kShutdownStagger = 0.12f;
  inline constexpr float kShutdownSpread = 3.0f;
  inline constexpr float kKingPause = 0.8f;
  inline constexpr float kCollapseSeconds = 0.7f;

  /// When each standing piece's projector shuts down, in seconds since the decision.
  /// `pieces` lists the standing pieces with their side, royal flag and distance from
  /// the origin; the result is parallel to it.
  struct ShutdownPiece { Color side; bool royal; int dist; };
  [[nodiscard]] std::vector<float> shutdownStarts(EndFx end, std::span<const ShutdownPiece>,
                                                  int maxDist, float intensity);

  /// One piece's hologram at `age` seconds after its own start.
  struct HologramShutdown { float opacity; float reveal; float scanJitter; float baseHeat; };
  [[nodiscard]] HologramShutdown hologramShutdown(float age, std::uint32_t seed,
                                                  float intensity);
  /// When the last projector is out, so the standby LED and the HUD know.
  [[nodiscard]] float shutdownComplete(...);
  ```
- `src/render/board_fx.cpp`: in the `endAge` branch, after `hologramEnd`, compose
  `hologramShutdown` for pieces whose start has passed. Opacity and scan jitter multiply;
  reveal and heat write the existing `CellFx` fields. No new `CellFx` fields are
  needed; it stays at 48 bytes.
- `src/render/board_renderer.cpp`: the standby LED instance, emitted while
  `BoardFxState` reports the shutdown complete. Its position is the bezel corner nearest
  the winner's home rank on the slab that holds the origin cell.
- The move-hold for a game that ends by a move (`applyMoveHold`) must still finish the
  move before anything shuts down. The 3 s delay already covers this; add a test.

### Debug access

- Existing `endfx win|draw|off [white|black]` and `--end-t` now show the shutdown too.
  Update their text in `help()` and `docs/debug-console.md`: "the hold is reached by
  about 9" becomes the new end time, roughly 12 to 13 s for a full army.
- New: `--end-t` past the shutdown time shows the dark slab and the standby LED.

### Tests

- Unit (`[view]`):
  - order is loser farthest first, loser royal, winner nearest first, winner royal last;
  - the king pause is there;
  - the spread cap holds for 32 pieces;
  - draw interleaves;
  - calm uses a 0.05 s stagger and no pause;
  - `hologramShutdown` has opacity 0 and reveal 0 at age ≥ 0.7, and is identical for an
    equal seed.
- GPU: at `--end-t` 3.5 every piece is visible; at the computed complete time no piece
  instance has opacity above 0 and the standby instance exists. Undo after the end
  restores opacity 1 and reveal ≥ 1 at power-up age 0.5.
- gui ctest `gui-end-shutdown`: a fool's-mate script, captures at `--end-t` 3.0 / 6.0 /
  9.0 / 11.0 / 14.0. Each differs from the previous; 14.0 has no hologram pixels above a
  threshold in the piece bands, except the standby LED.
- Contact sheet in both themes, decisive and draw, with intensity 1 and 0.
