## What this is

M24.5 deploys up to 4 mast groups with travel and anything bigger statically. After this
slice:
1. **Every shipped variant** deploys and stows with travel, with no static fallback. One
   carriage per mast group, routed so that carriages and masts never collide, with
   departures overlapping so a deploy's slab phase stays within 1.6 s.
2. **Layout changes move the hardware.** Changing the view (grep the ViewConfig changes
   the player can make: axis assignment, flat ↔ stacked, the layout toggles) makes the
   gantry carry each slab from its old pose to its new one. Nothing teleports.
3. **5D:** a new timeline's slab arrives from the rack and boots beside its parent,
   joined to it by a **light-guide cable** (`TimelineLink`). When the multiverse
   outgrows the volume, `labScale` eases over 1 s and the masts re-space.
4. **Light bridges:** sub-boards that are glued along a grid axis but cannot touch in
   3-D (the last level of a wrapping stack and its first; a periodic timeline) are joined
   by **beams of light** in that seam's axis colour. A piece crossing such a gluing
   travels **along the bridge** as a hologram.
5. **Stacked D ≥ 3 shape views** (`torus3d*`, `hyper4*`, `t6`: the `stacked()` shapes,
   where cells become a cloud of tiles in a 3-D/4-D projection) cannot be hardware. In
   their **shape** view, the slabs rise to the gantry's top and dim to standby, and the
   **bench projector** (or the M24.4c wall projector if nearer) shows the shape as light,
   with the current hologram material. Returning to flat lowers them again. This is the
   only place the board leaves its slabs: hardware where possible, light where
   impossible.

## Step 1 — carriage routing (pure, test first)

In `src/view/rig_deploy.*` (extend; keep M24.5's API working):

1. **Bridges are shared by column.** A carriage hangs between two bridges spanning Y and
   riding the X rails (M24.5 copies the template per group). Carriages whose x are
   within 0.34 m share one bridge pair. Different columns need ≥ 0.34 m between their x.
   Carriages on the same bridge need ≥ 0.32 m between their y.
2. **Merging.** Mast groups closer than these spacings are merged into one **rack
   mast**: one mast, with a horizontal **T-bar** at its foot (a `bevel` box along the
   axis joining the merged groups' attachment points), and the yokes hang from the T-bar
   rather than from the mast. Write
   `std::vector<MastGroup> routeGroups(std::vector<MastGroup>, const DeployInfo&)`, which
   merges greedily by distance until every spacing rule holds.
   - **Tests:**
     - every shipped variant (iterate `variants/*.toml` via the existing variant loader;
       grep how the property tests enumerate variants) in its default view gives groups
       that satisfy all spacing rules;
     - no two masts' cylinders (Ø 0.05 m), T-bars or yokes intersect;
     - standard and cube5 are unchanged from M24.5 (one group).
3. **Departure order:** groups depart in order of their distance from the magazine
   (nearest first), at `0.2 + i · step` with `step = min(0.12, 1.2 / max(1, n − 1))`, so
   the last departure is ≤ 1.4 s. The slab path (M24.5 `slabDeploy`) is unchanged. Two
   carriages on one bridge move as one: the bridge's x is the shared x, and their y moves
   happen on the same timeline.
4. **Remove the static fallback** of M24.5 (design decision 5) once all variants pass.
   Keep calm intensity's no-travel path.

## Step 2 — layout changes move the hardware

1. When the layout key changes during a game (M24.5 caches `RigXform` per layout key),
   compute the old and new slab poses (centre and orientation per slice). Run a
   **relayout move**: per slab, `slabRelayout(old, new, t)`:
   - lift 0.08 m (0.15 s), travel along the rails (rigEase over 0.5–0.9 s depending on
     distance), turn about Z to the new yaw if it differs (during travel), lower, and
     settle (M24.5's overshoot);
   - slabs that exist only in the new layout come from the rack (`slabDeploy`); slabs
     that exist only in the old one return to it (stow path);
   - the board's cells for a moving slab follow it: the slab is powered, so the real
     board instances move, **not a stand-in**. Apply a per-slice transform to the board
     instances of that slice: translation plus yaw, applied in `buildInstances`' output
     by slice (find how instances know their slice; M24.5 found a way for visibility).
2. `labScale` changes ease over **1.0 s** with `rigEase`, during the same move.
3. **Picking during a relayout:** disabled (input is blocked until the move ends;
   skippable).
4. **Tests:** `slabRelayout` endpoints exact; continuity; a slice's transform at t = 0
   and t = end is identity relative to the old and new layouts.

## Step 3 — 5D: arrivals and light-guide cables

1. When `Session::timelineLinks()` grows (a new branch), the new timeline's slab
   arrives: `slabDeploy` from the next free bay to its layout position, and it boots on
   lock (per-slab boot: if the boot is global today, gate only the new slice's cells
   with their own boot age. Grep how the boot age is applied per cell in
   `applyBoardFx`, and add a per-slice boot-age override).
2. **The cable:** for each `TimelineLink`, a cable from the parent's mast to the child's
   mast at 0.15 m above the slabs' top, sagging (a catenary approximated by 12
   segments, sag = 8 % of the span). Material glass (3), colour `#9FE8FF`, with a moving
   light pulse along it (glow material dots, 3 per cable, speed 0.6 m/s) while either
   board is powered.
3. If the layout's bounds outgrow the volume, `labScale` eases (Step 2.2) and the masts
   re-space via a relayout move.
4. **Tests:** one arrival per new branch; the cable endpoints sit on the two masts; the
   sag is within tolerance.

## Step 4 — light bridges

1. **Where (pure):**
   ```cpp
   struct LightBridge { std::uint32_t sliceA, sliceB; std::uint8_t axis; view::Rgba color;
                        view::Vec3 a, b; };   ///< board space, the two edge midpoints
   [[nodiscard]] std::vector<LightBridge> lightBridges(const VariantSpec& v,
                                                       const ViewConfig& cfg,
                                                       const std::vector<Placement>& p,
                                                       const view::Theme& theme);
   ```
   A bridge exists for each pair of slices that the variant glues along a **grid** axis
   (not one of the two drawn screen axes; those are the in-slab seams) where the two
   slices are **not spatially adjacent** in the layout (their facing edges are more than
   1.5 cells apart). Find the gluings by asking the topology which cells are neighbours
   across the grid axis (grep how `SeamMap::build` asks for screen-axis neighbours, and
   do the same for grid axes). The colour is the seam ramp colour for that axis
   (`seamRampColor` or the axis colour the legend uses). `a` and `b` are the midpoints of
   the two slabs' facing edges: the edge on the side of the gluing.
2. **Look:** a beam from `a` to `b`, a slightly arched quadratic Bézier with its control
   point 0.25 × span above the midpoint, so it never cuts through a slab between them. Draw
   it as 16 segments of the `cylinder` mesh, Ø 0.01 m, **hologram material (4)**, the
   colour at alpha 0.55, plus a faint glow core (glow material, Ø 0.004 m). It is
   emitted by the slab edges, so it fades with the board's power.
3. **Pieces crossing:** when a move's `MovePath` (`src/view/move_anim.hpp`) goes from a
   cell on slice A to a cell on slice B across a light bridge (the move crosses that
   gluing), the animated piece follows the **bridge curve** instead of the straight
   chord:
   - from its start cell to `a` (the first 20 % of the move's time);
   - along the Bézier (60 %);
   - from `b` to its end cell (20 %).
   While on the bridge, it is drawn with its hologram scan weight raised to 1 (scan-only,
   `panelSignal[3]` per the Instance comment for hologram pieces) and is stretched 1.3×
   along the path. Find where the move animation computes the piece's position (grep
   `MovePath` consumers in `board_renderer.cpp`), and add the bridge path as a
   `MovePath` variant (`bridge` index) set by the session when it builds the path.
4. **Tests:**
   - `torus3d` (levels wrap) in a stacked layout: one bridge per wrapping pair, between
     the first and last level;
   - `cube5`: none (nothing glued that doesn't touch);
   - a bridge's control point is above both endpoints;
   - a crossing move's piece position at 0, 0.2, 0.5, 0.8 and 1.0 of its time is the
     start, `a`, the Bézier middle, `b` and the end.

## Step 5 — stacked shape views as light

1. When the session's geometry view is on (`geometryFormed > 0`) and the surface is
   `stacked()`:
   - the slabs **rise** to the gantry's top (mast shortened to 0.15 m) and power to
     standby (the M23.12 standby LED). Use the relayout machinery with "parked high"
     poses. Their cells are not drawn as LEDs;
   - the shape's tiles (today drawn as LED patches) are drawn with **hologram material**
     at the current transform, centred where the layout was. Reuse M24.5b's hologram
     band path (Step 5.2 there): the same pipeline and the same opacity convention, at
     opacity 0.8;
   - the projector nearest to the layout centre (bench or wall) shows a **beam** to the
     shape's bounds (M24.3 `projectorBeam`, generalised to any direction in M24.4c);
   - picking keeps working (the shape's pick is `PlaySurface::pick`, unchanged).
2. Returning to flat reverses it: tiles fade, slabs lower and power up (a short boot,
   0.4 s, the calm boot form), and cells draw as LEDs again.
3. **Tests:** a GPU test where the stacked shape view, lab on, has no LED-material patches
   and some hologram patches, and the slab masts are at their short length.

Deviations and decisions:
1. **`routeGroups` takes an extra `labScale` parameter** (default 1): the metre rules need
   it to convert to board units, and the `DeployInfo` alone does not carry it.
2. **Same-column rule:** two carriages share a bridge only at the same x (1 mm); masts at
   different x closer than 0.34 m, or on one bridge closer than 0.32 m in y, merge. With
   the shipped 0.1 m grid gaps this makes hyper4 one rack (16 slabs), hyper4_8x8 two, and
   t6 one rack of 256 slabs (64 slices × 4 levels) at labScale 1.66.
3. **Rack geometry** (arms in the gaps, spine in a column gap) is this slice's own design:
   the spec's "a T-bar at its foot" put bars under neighbouring slabs on 2-D grids (the
   every-variant intersection test caught it on t6).
4. **A relayout's cells:** a view change regroups cells into different slabs, so a slab's
   cells cannot all start where they were. Each new slab is matched to the old slab it
   shares most cells with and starts exactly at that slab's pose (centre, yaw); its own
   cells ride it rigidly. The test checks that, the identity at the end, continuity and
   the calm form. A pure mirror mapping (e.g. `axes rank,file`) gets yaw 0.
5. **Light bridges on a level stack** (torus3d) join the slabs' near (−Y) edges and arch
   towards the player by a quarter of the span: for a vertical pair "above the midpoint"
   would cut through the levels between, and the −X side hid the beam behind the stack.
   The "control above both endpoints" test therefore holds for side-by-side bridges (t6's
   grid axes); the stack's bridge is tested to stand off and arch outward. `LightBridge`
   also carries `slabA` / `slabB` and its `control` point (a 3-D board's levels are one
   slice but separate slabs). The colour is `seamRampColor(theme, axis/(dims−1))`.
6. **Bridges show once every slab is clamped**, fading in with the boot through the board
   power; they are not drawn during a relayout or a stacked shape view.
7. **5d capture:** the capture tooling cannot easily script a time-travel branch, so
   `gui-lab-multislab` and `5d-branch.png` use an ordinary first move, which also adds a
   board (the next turn) and exercises the same arrival, boot-on-lock and cable code. A
   branch's cable is covered by the pure test.
8. **Out-of-bezel board instances** (5d's time rail and arrow) now ride the nearest slab
   in `carryBoard` / `carryRelayout`; before, the arrow hung in place while its slab
   travelled.
9. Existing test changed: `stow reverses the path, last group first` selected groups by
   slice order; M24.6's departures go by distance from the magazine, so it now selects by
   departure slot. `appendRibs` was refactored onto the new `cylinderBetween` /
   `insertLabCylinders` (same instances). `clang-format` reformatted a few pre-existing
   unformatted lines in the touched files (`board_fx.*`, `test_board_fx.cpp`).
10. The stacked shape's hologram reads dim (the cells' own checker colours through the
    hologram shading at 0.8 screen door); the beam from the projector is faint at
    `theme.ember × 0.12`. Left as specified.
11. t6's deploy fills the bench view with 256 slabs leaving the 8 bays at once (one rack
    departs in one slot); physically honest, visually busy.
