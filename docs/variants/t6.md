# t6 - a six-dimensional torus

Definition: [`variants/t6.toml`](../../variants/t6.toml)

Six axes of four cells each, every face glued to its opposite. The board is a T⁶:
4096 cells, no edge anywhere, and every cell identical to every other - a rook's line
is a closed loop of four, a bishop's diagonal closes on itself, and nothing can be
cornered.

This is the compactification the engine's geometry can actually express. The main
menu draws a Calabi-Yau quintic, and a torus *is* a Calabi-Yau - the flat, toroidal
one. The smoothed quintic itself is not a quotient of a box by face identifications:
the geometry layer glues faces of a flat lattice and derives direction transport from
an affine transition group (ADR-0004), which reaches the projective plane no better
than it reaches a quintic threefold (the expressiveness gap recorded in
[M3](../plan/M3-geometry.md)). T⁶ is the string-theory compactification that is pure
data.

## The algebra at six axes

The pieces are the ordinary vector-move atoms, and the dimension does the rest. An
atom of `k` distinct magnitudes is placed on `k` distinct axes in every signed order,
so the direction count is `P(6,k)·2^k`:

| Piece | Atom | Directions |
|---|---|---|
| rook | `[1]` | `P(6,1)·2 = 12` |
| knight | `[1,2]` | `P(6,2)·4 = 120` |
| bishop | `[1,1]` | `P(6,2)·4 = 120` |
| queen | rook + bishop | 132 |
| king | `[1]` + `[1,1]` | 12 + 120 = 132 |

A king moving to any of its 132 neighbours is the single number that best says what
six dimensions mean for play; a knight's 120 destinations is the other. On a 4-cell
torus a slider cannot travel far before wrapping onto itself, so the branching comes
from the *width* of the direction set, not the length of any one ray.

## Rules, and which are our own choice

**Rules:** EndspielZero's own; there is no external definition of six-torus chess to
follow. The choices:

- **No promotion, no castling.** Every axis is a loop, so there is no last rank and
  no unambiguous space "between" a king and a rook.
- **Pawns are declared but not fielded.** The board is orientable, so `forward` along
  `x1` is globally definable and a pawn is meaningful in an edited position; but it
  can never promote here, so the opening omits pawns the way the Klein bottle variant
  omits every oriented piece.
- **A small opening array** - king, rook and knight a side - placed close together.
  A full array is unplayable for the usual glued-board reason (the two back ranks are
  in contact), and at six axes it is also unaffordable to *search*: a king alone has
  132 moves, so each added piece multiplies an already-large branching factor. The
  full piece set stays declared so a position can contain any of it.
